# Library Streaming Passes

The single most underused free-music trick in existence: **your public library card**. Libraries pay real licensing fees so their patrons can stream (and sometimes download-keep) enormous catalogs — full modern albums, classical labels, scores. If your library offers these and you're not using them, you're leaving free music on the table.

## Hoopla

**[hoopladigital.com](https://www.hoopladigital.com/)**

The friend of this whole directory. Hoopla partners with libraries across the US, Canada, and beyond to give you a monthly allotment of borrows across full music albums, movies, ebooks, and comics. The music catalog is genuinely deep — current pop and rock alongside classical, jazz, and soundtracks. You can stream in the browser or via their mobile app, and the experience is clean and ad-free because it's your library paying for it, not an ad network.

*How to start:* create an account, pick your library from the list, and enter your card number. Your library's specific collection may vary, which is normal.

**The Fine Print:**
- You need a **valid library card** from a participating library — Hoopla does nothing without it.
- Your borrows are capped per month (often 5–30, set by your library). Albums count as one borrow each.
- Some libraries simply don't include Hoopla at all. If yours doesn't, peek at [Freegal](#freegal) instead.

## Freegal Music

**[freegalmusic.com](https://www.freegalmusic.com/)**

Freegal's party trick is different from Hoopla's: every week you get a handful of **MP3 downloads that are yours to keep forever**, no due date, no return. It catalogs a big slice of Sony Music (huge label, so most popular artists are there) plus older catalogs you'd need a record store with a time machine to find. Streams are unlimited; the keep-forever downloads are the rationed treats.

*How to start:* the "Find My Library" search on the homepage walks you through it in under a minute.

**The Fine Print:**
- Library card required, from a **Freegal-participating** library specifically.
- Download allotments are small and depend on your library's tier — the classic setup is 5 MP3 downloads a week, some libraries offering 3. Your account dashboard shows your exact number.
- Streaming is typically unlimited, so a smart play is: stream freely all week, then spend your weekly downloads on the songs you actually want to own.

## Naxos Music Library

**[naxosmusiclibrary.com/home](https://www.naxosmusiclibrary.com/home)**

The classical-lover's lane. Naxos licenses 200+ classical, jazz, and world-music labels into library and institutional subscriptions — complete albums by major orchestras, plus educational extras like curated playlists. It's the library-branded doorway to one of the deepest classical catalogs anywhere.

*How to start:* ask your library whether it subscribes (check the "databases" or "research" page of your library's site, where it often appears as "Naxos Music Library"). If it does, log in with your card and you get full access ahead of anything else in this category except maybe Hoopla.

**The Fine Print:**
- Full catalog access requires a **library subscription**, and coverage is uneven — big city systems often have it, small systems often don't.
- Concurrent-listener limits exist per library, so peak evening hours can occasionally put you in a queue.
