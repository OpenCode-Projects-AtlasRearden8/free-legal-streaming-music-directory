# Royalty-Free Players

Music you're **licensed to use** in your own videos, podcasts, games, or small-business promos — free. Which is a different question from free to listen, so the fine print is the star of this page. Rules of thumb: read each license yourself before a commercial project, and keep receipts (a download log with license text saved) in case you're ever asked.

## Free Music Archive (FMA)

**[freemusicarchive.org](https://freemusicarchive.org/)**

A curated catalog of tracks posted by independent artists, with clear license labels (CC BY, CC BY-SA, etc.) so you know, before you download, whether attribution is required. Founded in 2009, now run by WFMU — New Jersey's finest freeform station — so curation is real. Browse by genre (jazz, pop, classical, hip-hop) or filter by license type.

**The Fine Print:**
- Licenses vary per track. Your project's host (YouTube, podcast distributors, storefronts) has its own content-ID claims machinery; some CC tracks can still nudge automated systems.
- A subscriptions/tier exists for commercial-safe blanket licensing; free CC tracks don't need it — but read.

## ccMixter

**[ccmixter.org](https://ccmixter.org/)**

The remix-village of the Creative Commons world: musicians upload stems and mixes for others to remix, and everything lands under explicit CC licenses. A great, genuinely fun place for unusual background music (and a good intro to "what is a CC license, anyway").

**The Fine Print:**
- Licenses vary track by track. Most are CC BY (credit the artist and you're fine), but some are CC BY-SA or CC BY-NC — NonCommercial means your monetized YouTube channel does *not* qualify, so read the label before you fall in love with a track.
- Expect a real range of production values; it's a community rather than a library.

## Pixabay Music

**[pixabay.com/music](https://pixabay.com/music/)**

Pixabay — properly well known for free stock content — has a big royalty-free music library. And for a beginner, Pixabay's pitch might win: no attribution required, no account needed to download, license terms written in a single paragraph of plain English. Mood and genre filters work well. "Free for commercial use, no attribution needed" is the first sentence, politely.

**The Fine Print:**
- Not CC licenses — Pixabay's own "Content License." For most personal and small-business uses that's even more comfortable than CC.
- Pixabay blocks automated tools (our verification script gets a 403 from it; browsers usually don't). If it won't open for you either, try a normal home network or a different browser.
- Watch out for "content-ID" style claims: some stock tracks elsewhere are relisted YouTube-claimed; audit carefully if your channel's been flagged before.

## Incompetech

**[incompetech.com/music/royalty-free](https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/)**

Kevin MacLeod's own stock-music shop: thousands of compositions (the internet-famous "Sneaky Snitch," "Carefree," and about ten others you'd recognize from a movie trailer in a second) — searchable by mood, feel, and genre. Countless indie films, games, and podcasts have shipped with his music. It works via CC BY: free, even commercially, if you credit *"Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), Licensed under Creative Commons: By Attribution 4.0."*

**The Fine Print:**
- Attribution is required. If you can't spare even the smallest credit line, there's a paid license option that removes it.
- This is a personal archive run by one composer — no fancy player apps, just an old-school site and direct MP3s.

## Bensound

**[bensound.com](https://www.bensound.com/)**

Polished royalty-free stock library that's been a go-to for YouTube and small businesses for years. Free-tier tracks are solid, well-produced pieces, and browsing by mood or intended use is genuinely beginner-friendly.

**The Fine Print:**
- The free license is CC-BY-style: attribution to "Bensound" required on published works, and use is limited to everyday productions (web videos, podcasts, small projects). Check their current terms before anything commercial — they revisit limits occasionally.
- Full commercial use (products, ads, bigger campaigns) requires one of their paid licenses. The free tier is a starter tier: honest about it.

## Musopen

**[musopen.org](https://musopen.org/)**

Classical recordings of public-domain works, from a nonprofit whose mission is literally "making music accessible" — they've recorded and released entire catalogs of classical music into free circulation. Browse by composer, instrument, or period. They also host public-domain sheet music. A nonprofit mission with real recording quality: an odd and wonderful pairing.

**The Fine Print:**
- Free accounts stream freely; some downloads (or higher-quality files) gate behind a membership tier.
- Like Pixabay, Musopen blocks automated tools (our verification script gets a 403). Reach it in a normal browser; if it refuses you too, try again from a regular home connection.
- Performance copyright subsists even when the *composition* is public domain. Musopen licenses what they distribute; double-check the "usage license" note under any track you publish with.
