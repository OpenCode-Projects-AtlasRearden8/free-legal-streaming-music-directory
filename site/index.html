The Free & Legal Streaming Music Directory

Warm, honest guides to hearing music and calm audio without paying a subscription: library streaming passes, public-radio apps, live coding-game stations, archives of older music, and royalty-free players.

1 Library streaming passes

The most underused free-music trick in existence: your public library card. Libraries pay real licensing fees so you can stream — and sometimes keep — enormous catalogs.

Hoopla

hoopladigital.com

Full music albums (plus movies, ebooks, comics) borrowed through your library — clean, ad-free, browser or app. A genuinely deep catalog spanning pop, classical, jazz, and soundtracks.

Fine print: needs a valid library card from a participating library; monthly borrows are capped (often 5–30, set by your library).

Freegal Music

freegalmusic.com

A big slice of Sony Music, with weekly MP3 downloads you keep forever, no due date. Unlimited streaming plus a ration of keep-for-keeps — a lovely two-speed model.

Fine print: library card from a Freegal-participating library required; downloads are rationed (classically 5 MP3s a week).

Naxos Music Library

naxosmusiclibrary.com

The classical-lover's lane: 200+ labels' complete albums licensed to library systems, with curated playlists, jazz, and world music in the mix. Log in with your card and it all opens.

Fine print: your library must subscribe; coverage skews to bigger systems, and peak hours can queue you.

2 Public-radio apps and streams

Listener-funded radio: donations instead of interruptions. These are the stations people leave on for entire workdays.

NPR

npr.org

Tasteful music curation, live concerts, and sessions, with the free NPR One app for gentle radio listening. Home base for the whole public-radio way of hearing.

Fine print: curated segments rather than full-album play; the app will ask, politely, for donations.

KEXP

kexp.org/listen

Seattle's beloved listener-powered station: DJs with real taste and thousands of live in-studio sessions archived free on YouTube. Weekday highs, 1990s weekends, global electronic shows.

Fine print: frequent funding drives (they're listener-funded by design); YouTube session archive carries YouTube ads.

YourClassical

yourclassical.org

Classical Minnesota Public Radio's calm-audio gem: free 24/7 streams including "Peaceful Classical" and piano-only channels — a perfect low-effort focus soundtrack.

Fine print: continuous mixes only, no on-demand picking; the mobile app is smoother than the bare browser stream.

WFMT

wfmt.com

Chicago's classical flagship: long-form programming and opera broadcasts with real announcers telling you what you just heard. Free web stream, respectful old-school radio.

Fine print: long-form means long — an evening opera is a commitment; listener-support mentions instead of ads.

Radio Garden

radio.garden

A spinning globe covered in live radio stations. Drag the earth, land somewhere, and instantly hear local radio — world music and voices you'd never find on any app chart.

Fine print: individual stations wobble in and out; it streams constantly, so mind your phone data plan.

3 Live coding-game stations

Stations built around what you're doing: coding, gaming, deep focus. Community-voted game music, endless lofi, noncommercial chill radio.

Rainwave

rainwave.cc

Community interactive radio for video-game and anime music: songs spin up by live community vote, and the chat debates the canon while you code or game. A station you participate in.

Fine print: the crowd controls the queue — trust the democracy or wait out a losing vote; requests and voting work best with a (free) account.

SomaFM

somafm.com

Noncommercial, listener-supported radio since 2000, with 30+ handcrafted channels. Groove Salad is the legendary ambient work companion — put it on for eight hours and forget it exists.

Fine print: twice-yearly pledge drives and honest requests for listener money — that's the entire catch. No accounts, no ads.

Chillhop Music

chillhop.com

The famous chillhop/lofi radio built on a real, licensed catalog of their own — browsable by mood ("chill study beats," "sleepy," "jazzy & mellow"). Independent team, quality catalog since 2013.

Fine print: the YouTube version carries standard ads (their own web player doesn't); it's continuous radio — no skipping.

LoFi Café

lofi.cafe

A quiet, gorgeous minimal website that is nothing but an endless lofi radio stream with a volume button. The zero-click, zero-login calm stream for when you just opened your laptop.

Fine print: one channel only — if lofi is not the calm flavor you wanted, it's a hard no.

Lofi Girl (YouTube live)

youtube.com/@LofiGirl

The famous "beats to relax/study to" livestream, running around the clock since 2017: a girl studying with her cat, and beats. Hundreds of millions of study hours can't be wrong.

Fine print: it's YouTube, so periodic ads are the honest price; you can't skip or queue.

OverClocked ReMix

ocremix.org

Thousands of peer-reviewed, fan-made remixes of video-game music — ~25 years of a fan project that became free art — with built-in streaming playlists you steer yourself.

Fine print: the original music still belongs to the game studios: free to listen, stream, and download, not licensed for commercial use.

4 Archives for older music

Historical, out-of-copyright, and public-domain recordings. Nobody is selling anything here — libraries and archives putting the past online for free.

Internet Archive — Audio

archive.org/details/audio

A deep ocean of legal free audio: concert archives built on each band's taping policy, historical collections, radio programs, spoken word. Millions of items, streamed right in the browser.

Fine print: rights vary item by item — superb for listening, but clear rights before commercial reuse.

IA 78 RPM Records

archive.org/details/78rpm

400,000+ digitized shellac records from 1898–1950s: jazz, blues, opera, comedy — the Great 78 Project. If you've wondered what the Gramophone era sounded like, here it is, crackles and all.

Fine print: pre-1924 US recordings are solidly public domain; later ones are mostly free to listen but not always free to reuse.

IA Live Music Archive

archive.org/details/etree

Band-approved concert recordings — thousands of Grateful Dead shows plus hundreds of bands that explicitly allow audience taping. Full shows, free to stream.

Fine print: stream-vs-download rights depend on each band's policy; sound quality varies wildly (read the notes).

National Jukebox

loc.gov/collections/national-jukebox

The Library of Congress streaming tens of thousands of Victor, Columbia, and Edison-era discs from 1901–1925, with museum-grade metadata. The "hear the story behind the song" archive.

Fine print: streaming only — no downloads, and the player is an archive tool rather than a slick modern app.

IMSLP

imslp.org

The great public-domain music library: hundreds of thousands of classical scores plus a large collection of performances of those works, from contributors worldwide.

Fine print: country-specific recording licenses (USA-only vs. worldwide labels); ads with a modest paid tier to remove them.

5 Royalty-free players

Music you're licensed to use in your own videos, podcasts, and projects — free. Read each license before a commercial project, and keep receipts.

Free Music Archive

freemusicarchive.org

Curated, clearly-labeled tracks from independent artists (CC BY, BY-SA, etc.), now run by WFMU. Browse by genre or filter by license so you know what you agreed to before you download.

Fine print: licenses vary per track — CC BY-NC tracks won't fit a monetized channel; hosts' content-ID systems can still flag some tracks.

ccMixter

ccmixter.org

The remix village of the Creative Commons world: musicians upload stems and mixes for others to remix, everything under explicit CC licenses. Genuine fun, unusual finds.

Fine print: license type varies by track (BY, SA, NC); production values range widely — it's a community, not a polished library.

Pixabay Music

pixabay.com/music

A big royalty-free library with the friendliest starter terms in this category: free for commercial use, no attribution required, plain-English license, no account needed to download.

Fine print: Pixabay's own license (not CC); it 403-blocks automation — try a normal browser if it refuses you too.

Incompetech

incompetech.com

Kevin MacLeod's shop of thousands of compositions ("Sneaky Snitch," "Carefree"…) — searchable by mood, and the soundtrack of a generation of indie films and games. CC BY: free, even commercially, with credit.

Fine print: attribution required (a paid license removes it if lines of credit offend you); old-school site, direct MP3s.

Bensound

bensound.com

Polished royalty-free stock music that's been a go-to for YouTube and small businesses for years, with genuinely beginner-friendly browsing by mood or intended use.

Fine print: the free license is CC-BY-style with required credit, limited to everyday productions; bigger commercial uses need paid licenses.

Musopen

musopen.org

Classical recordings of public-domain works, recorded and freed by a nonprofit with exactly that mission — plus public-domain sheet music. An odd and wonderful pairing.

Fine print: some downloads gate behind a membership; performance copyrights persist even when compositions are PD; it 403-blocks automation too.
